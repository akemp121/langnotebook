# Spanish Beginner Core — Chapter Map

**Status:** Structure approved in principle; chapter contents and source mapping are still to be developed. Each chapter is planned as one ~15-minute notebook lesson. Thirty chapters give approximately 7.5 hours of core instruction, not fluency; ongoing practice and a later intermediate path will be needed.

The order prioritizes sentence construction, early person-by-person verb forms, useful questions, and frequent function words. Function words and connectors should recur across chapters rather than appear only in a vocabulary dump. Exact examples, explanations, activities, and source citations will be determined during lesson-by-lesson design.

## Build the basics

1. **Spanish sounds and spelling** — Alphabet, pronunciation, syllables, and stress.
2. **How a Spanish sentence is built** — Basic word order and sentence parts.
3. **Nouns, gender, articles, and plurals** — *el/la, un/una* and basic agreement.
4. **Who’s doing what? Pronouns and verb persons** — Person/number grid and subject pronouns.
5. **The verb *ser*** — Core person forms; identity and basic descriptions.
6. **Ask the essentials** — *qué, quién, dónde, cuándo, cómo, por qué, cuál,* and *cuánto*.
7. **Yes, no, and not** — *sí/no*, negation, and short responses.
8. **Regular present-tense *-ar* verbs** — Full person grid in simple sentences.
9. **Regular present-tense *-er* and *-ir* verbs** — Compare the patterns.
10. **Describing people and things** — Adjective forms and agreement.

## Make useful sentences

11. **Where things are: *estar* and *hay*** — Location and existence.
12. **The high-use verb *tener*** — Possession, age, and common expressions.
13. **Going places: *ir* and *a*** — Destinations and near-future plans.
14. **Possession and pointing things out** — Possessives and demonstratives.
15. **The small words that connect sentences** — *y, o, pero, porque, de, en,* and *con*.
16. **Time, dates, and numbers** — Common time, date, quantity, and price questions.
17. **Likes and preferences: *gustar*** — A useful pattern that differs from English.
18. **Everyday actions and reflexive verbs** — Daily routines, with verb forms across persons.
19. **Talking about actions happening now** — Present progressive.
20. **Requests and simple instructions** — Useful commands and polite requests.

## Talk about more than the present

21. **The preterite: completed past actions** — Begin with regular forms and person grids.
22. **Common irregular past forms** — Prioritize high-frequency verbs.
23. **The imperfect: habits and background in the past.**
24. **Choosing between the preterite and imperfect.**
25. **Talking about future plans** — Extend *ir a* and introduce other useful future forms as appropriate.
26. **Comparisons, quantities, and degree** — *más, menos, muy,* and related patterns.
27. **Pronouns for “it,” “them,” and “to/for someone”** — Introduce object pronouns in useful patterns.
28. **Purpose, cause, and route: *por* and *para*** — Common contrasting uses.
29. **Linking ideas and keeping a conversation going** — Revisit connectors and high-value response/discourse words (for example, *ya, todavía, también, tampoco,* and *quizá*); label regional usage such as *ahorita* carefully.
30. **Putting it together** — Combine questions, verb forms, connectors, and everyday scenarios.

## Working assumptions

- At each relevant verb/tense introduction, show the useful forms side by side across persons; explain when subject pronouns can be omitted.
- The working direction is broadly Latin American Spanish, with *vosotros* identified as a Spain variant. Confirm the variety/pronoun policy before writing lessons.
- Word choices and examples are not finalized. Validate them against FSI and the frequency/sentence datasets; label regional variation and check source quality and attribution.
